Topic
This project examines judicial decisions concerning maintenance under Section 125 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, during the period specified in the assignment: 1 September 2021 to 31 August 2026. Section 125 is a summary social-justice remedy intended to prevent destitution by providing maintenance to eligible wives, children and parents. The topic is significant because courts must balance the statutory conditions, the parties’ financial circumstances and the protective purpose of the provision.
How we found the judgments
This draft dataset is based on the 20 entries supplied in the team's completed Excel workbook. The recorded case names, case numbers, dates, issue descriptions, holdings, precedent fields and source links have been carried forward from that workbook. The source-link column contains a mixture of Indian Kanoon pages, Indian Kanoon search-result URLs and one official Supreme Court PDF link. The assignment asks for official court copies wherever available. Accordingly, each link must be checked and replaced with the official judgment URL where one exists before final submission.
Scope limitation: the supplied workbook identifies the court as “Supreme Court of India and High Courts of India” and includes cases from more than one court. This does not satisfy the assignment's instruction to select one court. The charts below therefore describe the supplied mixed-court entries only; they must not be presented as findings about a single court. The team should select one court, verify the court of every included decision, and rebuild the dataset with 10–20 qualifying reasoned judgments from that court before treating this project as final.
What the data shows

The year chart counts the 20 entries by the year recorded in the workbook. It provides a basic view of the distribution of the team's collected material over time. The distribution should not be interpreted as the actual volume of all judgments delivered by any one court, because the current dataset combines courts and may include incomplete or unverified entries.

The outcome chart counts the outcome labels entered in the workbook, with capitalization standardized for counting. Several entries are coded as “Other”, and the workbook contains a mixture of appellate and revisional matters. The chart therefore reflects the team's present coding choices rather than a verified measure of success rates in Section 125 litigation. The operative part of each judgment should be checked before finalizing the outcome category.

The third chart summarizes the “Who won” field as entered. A substantial “Mixed or unclear” category indicates that the present coding does not consistently identify a single successful party. This is not necessarily a defect in every case: maintenance decisions can grant partial relief or issue directions affecting both sides. Nevertheless, the team should apply a consistent rule based on the operative order and explain ambiguous outcomes.
Limits
This dataset is a small, non-random collection and cannot establish the overall behaviour of the judiciary or the success rate of maintenance claims. It currently combines multiple courts, includes links that require official-source verification, and contains entries whose relevance and coding need review. The charts are descriptive summaries of the supplied spreadsheet, not causal or statistically representative findings.
AI-use disclosure
AI assistance was used to help organize the supplied spreadsheet, generate descriptive charts, prepare the HTML presentation and draft this README. AI-generated summaries or classifications should not be treated as substitutes for reading judgments. Team members must verify each judgment against its full text, confirm the court, date, citation, statutory provision, outcome and precedent, and replace non-official links with official court copies where available. The “Coded by” field must identify the team member who actually read each judgment; the current workbook's “Research update” entries do not meet that requirement.
